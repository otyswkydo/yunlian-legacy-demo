Dragon Rhythms Welcome Spring · Opera Opens the Year — Chinese Opera Masters Light Up a New Year Night in Toronto

  • admin
  • January 8, 2024
  • Chinese Opera, Music

Gongs and drums rang out and voices soared. On the evening of January 7, 2024, a grand opera gala blending tradition and modernity took place at the P.C. Ho Theatre of the Chinese Cultural Centre of Greater Toronto. Three nationally treasured artists from China performed alongside local opera talent, giving Canadian audiences a moving encounter with China's national art. With the New Year approaching and the charm of opera in the air, it was a cultural gathering — and a homecoming for the heart.

🌉 A Gathering of Masters, Singing a Bridge Between China and Canada

Part of the global “Happy Chinese New Year” cultural series, the concert was hosted by the Canada-China Opera Culture Exchange Association, planned by the Canada China Cultural and Economic Development Alliance, and title-sponsored by Ruibang Asset Management. Its theme was “Care, Connection and Peace.”

The performance brought together three leading opera artists:

Hu Wenge, the only male dan disciple personally taught by Mei Baojiu (son of Mei Lanfang), and a renowned male dan artist of the Beijing Peking Opera Company, embodying the supreme elegance of the Mei school;

Wu Qiong, a leading figure in Huangmei Opera, whose spring-like smile and flowing voice are an “opera memory” for many overseas Chinese;

Ma Li, a celebrated young Peking Opera artist, professor at the Central Academy of Drama and Plum Blossom Award winner — a martial role performer equally strong in singing and recitation, full of spirit.

Their arrival filled a cold winter night with the warmth of culture.

🎤 Many Voices in Harmony: A Cross-Genre Opera Feast

The program spanned Peking Opera, Huangmei Opera, Yue Opera, Yu Opera, Ping Opera, Flower Drum Opera and more. With classic pieces in many styles, it satisfied older audiences' longing for the sounds of home and drew many young fans and audiences of diverse backgrounds into the theatre.

On stage, characters leapt and voices soared; in the audience, applause and cheers never stopped. Especially during pieces such as “The Drunken Concubine” and “The Female Consort Prince,” the audience followed every twist of the story, tense one moment and delighted the next, immersed in the subtle, lingering charm of Chinese opera.

🤝 Many Communities Come Together to Build a New Platform for Cultural Exchange

The gala was not only an artistic showcase but also a celebration of cultural exchange. Representatives from government, the Chinese community, business and major media attended; guests including Richmond Hill Councillor Cui Binghui offered congratulations, and organizations such as the Federation of Chinese Canadian Hometown Associations and the New Power Entrepreneurs Club gave their active support.

In her remarks, Sophia Lian, Chair of the Canada-China Cultural and Economic Development Alliance, said: “We hope that through opera, a unique cultural medium, we can build a bridge of deeper understanding and communication between China and Canada.”

The concert showcased the timeless vitality of China's finest traditional arts and gave Chinese Canadians a strong sense of cultural belonging.

🎨 Let the Light of Art Reach the Depths of the Heart

From an heir of the Mei school to a Huangmei Opera star, from a young martial performer to local opera fans — that night, art traveled through time and culture crossed borders. Opera was no longer just “China on stage,” but a touch of Eastern poetry in the world's shared culture.

And so the spring of 2024 quietly arrived, to the sound of gongs, drums and song.
